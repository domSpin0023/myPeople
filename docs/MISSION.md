# Mission

## Organization and profit

The organization behind MyPeople exists to release the app and sustain it, not to maximize revenue.

- **Goal:** collect a profit in a non-intrusive, non-obnoxious way. No forced or unskippable ads (for example, 5-second full-screen ads), and nothing designed to interrupt or trap the user.
- **Priority order:** user experience and health come first. Profit is balanced against them, and when the two conflict, user health wins.
- **Very low profit is expected and preferred** if it means more healthy users. The business only needs to stay sustainable enough to keep the app running and improving.

## Principles

- **Monetization must never work against the user.** A revenue idea is rejected if it increases time spent in the app, pressures the user, or exploits attention.
- **Consistent with the scope.** The app is meant to point people toward real-life relationships, with reduced gamification and as little time on screen as possible (see [SCOPE.md](SCOPE.md)). Engagement-driven business models (ads, attention metrics, retention tricks) work against that.
- **Transparency.** Anything paid or sponsored is clearly labeled and easy to hide or opt out of (see "Suggestions" in [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md)).
- **Respect for personal data.** The app holds information about people in the user's life. That data is never sold, and revenue never depends on it.

## The one metric: time spent

The app asks the user for a single metric about their relationships: **time spent**. There are no scores, rankings, or other numbers to fill in.

Time is tracked through:

- **Calendar events:** time spent together, from events with that person or group.
- **Voice-journal entries:** time recorded by speaking, so the user spends less time on screen.
- **Gift entries:** the time a gift took, counted in three ways:
  - time spent finding or choosing it,
  - time spent making it,
  - time spent *earning the money* to pay for it (how much of their work it took to afford it).

The last one is intentional. It reminds users that money and time are the same resource, and that what they spend on a person is a piece of their life. That nudges them to think about where the rest of their time goes, and who it is spent on.

This fits the principles above: one simple, honest measure, nothing to game, and the focus stays on real time given to real people (see [SCOPE.md](SCOPE.md) on reduced gamification).
